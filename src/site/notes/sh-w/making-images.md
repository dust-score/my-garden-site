First of all, I had a long period of struggling with making images. It always felt like a kind of imposition onto the surface of something that was not a willing participant. Of course, I'm aware that it doesn't have to function that way, but maybe because of the digital interface, and so many other interfaces when dealing with imagery, there's the sense that you're not only making the thing, but then you're also selling it, reproducing it several times, maybe reproducing it quite uncritically, like on Instagram for example. And I was aware that each and every reproduction is an instance of reconstructing what the work actually is, is like a work for itself. And so, um, there were a lot of kind of stacked layers of dealing with imagery. And in each of these different aspects of the stack, the image held a different kind of weight, and was also more or less just surface. So while maybe the physical work itself is much more than surface, as soon as it's understood in the context of contemporary art, it is understood in the context of the digital surface or the screen, as well as many other contextualising factors. So I had a lot of difficulty with basically making pure image. I really struggled with it because it felt so facetious. What I gravitated towards as a way of relieving myself was to get closer and closer to the material of what I was actually making the image on. 

And, yeah, simultaneously, I was kind of feeling more and more relationship with the past. I don't know, I assume that probably many people feel this way, but for me, growing up, like, 15 years ago, maybe 15 to 18 years ago, in Berlin, I really felt like I was living in, like, a nothing time. As though there had been some cut off point where the present, the contemporary moment, segregated from the past. It was probably unconsciously associated with the transition from like black and white to color photography. Psychologically, unconsciously, it must have been related to to my own untethered existence as a second generation migrant with no clear cultural anchor. But the counterpart, outside in broader society, contained this omnipresent messaging that Germany had "learned from its past". So for a large part of my younger years I kind of lived in this understanding of the world as somehow expressing a process of progress, as having moved on from something. And as an adult now in the last couple of years, I came to feel that this was complete bullshit. 

It was a juvenile preset that derived from a propagandistic way of framing our contemporary, you know, quote unquote "modern existence" in order for us to not have the agency of understanding what kind of a continuity we have come from. So I've come to feel that much more than whatever it was that I used to think, which was that it was somehow progressive to have, you know, moved on from tradition, I now feel that it is the opposite, a kind of disempowerment, to separate people from their heritage and also from a genealogy of - how did things get to where they are? Like when I look at the world around me, what do I recognise from the past? And how do I understand how close to me that past actually is? I came to feel that that past is really, really close. It's just as close as if I touch my own finger. 

All of these ways of classifying fascism, as a random example, of kind of categorising political instances in a kind of idolatrous way, in a way serve to isolate one aspect from itself in order to obfuscate the continuity that those instances are embedded in. That's the sense that I developed. 

And so when it came to images, my interest naturally evolved into an interest in making these historical continuities palpable. For example, I would have a material that has experienced something, has a history, and that I could use as a medium - not necessarily needing to impose some kind of a political message or not needing to impose a narrative on it at all, not wanting to construct something in a rigid sense at all, but to work together with a material that has existed for longer than I have in my lifetime, because it exceeds me. 

I feel that a material like that carries an implicit notion of the continuous nature of reality in it, in that it has kind of gone through, for example different systems, systems which proclaimed themselves as distinct from each other, and yet integrated into each other, almost seamlessly, you know. And for this reason, I also find bureaucracy to be so fascinating. It's such a  German thing, this self-declared relationship with the notion of bureaucracy. There's something about it, the way in which, the personal is abstracted into a kind of quantitative set of data so that these personal aspects become data driven, and then you're kind of confronted with the inhumanity of that in the form of a person whom you meet in one of these offices, which are also very dehumanising somehow. And in general there's this, you know, frustration and resentment towards these institutions because it's dehumanising to have to basically grovel for, you know, the bare minimum that you need to survive. Even if people could also do other things in order to survive, and I'm sure many don't feel terrible about going to the Jobcenter, but in the moment that they go to this place, they're making themselves transparent. They're subjecting themselves, they're being subjected to a paternalistic state power, and it is a kind of devaluing experience to be in the place because it's ugly, it's reductive, it doesn't provide a context in which one would recognise oneself as worthy. When one is surrounded by such an environment, rather the environment reinforces a sense of bare minimum and of low value.. So basically subsistence. 

So yeah, I'm interested in this paternalistic kind of bureaucratic management of human existence and potential, as a juncture with the relationship with the past, a way of making the past present, kind of tangible without being didactic, without being a pedagogue, without even really imposing narrative, that's what's interesting for me. Yeah. And whether it's, like, the agency of objects, following Jane Bennett, I don't want to, necessarily, assume that if I put objects together in this or that way, then they will have this or that effect, that would be that would kind of be the next level. First of all, it's me wanting to feel a relationship with the past.
