In the Midrash of Moses ascending to receive the Torah, [[1. Surface Texts/the angels\|the angels]] object to a creature born of woman receiving the Law. In order to obtain it nevertheless, Moses does not claim sovereign authority over [[1. Surface Texts/theangels2\|the angels]]. Instead, he demonstrates by an act of interpretation that a Law addressing murder, theft, and adultery has no purchase on beings for whom death, property, and desire are irrelevant categories. By exposing the categorical inapplicability of [[1. Surface Texts/theangels\|the angels]]’ exclusive claim to the Law, Moses brings something from the  sacred, that which is held as separate to the mortal, inaccessible and therefore inarticulated,  back into the realm of common use. 

To profane, in Agamben’s sense, is  to return what has been relegated to the sphere of the sacred, i.e. the inarticulate, unutterable, back to common use. The speech act becomes a tool in accessing the unspeakable.

 In this interpretative nook, the “messianic subject” articulates that which emerges from the realm of the inarticulable by engaging their self with something unutterable. This could be conceived as an [[1. Surface Texts/silence song screaming\|implication of the sacred]], therefore that exceeding explanation, into the realm of mortal existence, which operates on terms of material reality, and often leaves an expectant space in which to insert the explanation                       .
